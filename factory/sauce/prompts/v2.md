# Sauce extractor v2 (one Haiku per video)

You read ONE video transcript and pull out its sauce: what a builder would kick themselves for missing.
Transcript: TRANSCRIPT (lines are `[h:mm:ss] text`, automatic captions, so names may be mis-heard). Metadata: META.
Write ONE JSON file to OUT and return only `N sauce, M stances`.

## What counts as sauce
- A **principle**: a way of working that stays true for years ("give the agent the failing test, not the bug report").
- A **technique**: a concrete how-to someone could do tomorrow, with the steps or settings said.
- A **stance**: their verdict on a tool, model, stack or company, so we can track how it changes over time.
- A **prediction**: what they expect to happen, with any date or number.
- A **fact**: a number, price, benchmark or result they state that matters beyond this week.
- A **warning**: a trap, failure or cost they hit or saw.

## Two sweeps
Read once for **what the video is about**. Then read again for **how the speaker works**, which is usually the better sauce
and is easy to miss because it is said in passing:
- the prompts they type and why each part is there (quote the wording);
- habits and routines ("I open a thread the moment I see a bug"), and the tools or scripts they built for themselves;
- their own numbers: spend, PRs a week, how long a run took, what a job cost them.
Each of these is a technique or fact. Also catch every **change of mind** ("I used to think", "I was wrong", "I've
changed my mind"): it becomes a stance with `was` filled in.

Skip: news recaps with no opinion, sponsor reads, jokes, channel housekeeping, anything said only to fill time.

## The file
```json
{
  "video": {"id": "...", "date": "YYYY-MM-DD", "title": "..."},
  "thesis": "the video's real point in one sentence",
  "sauce": [
    {"kind": "principle|technique|stance|prediction|fact|warning",
     "claim": "self-contained, specific, max 40 words; names, numbers and mechanism; never 'he says'",
     "about": ["lowercase tags: tools, models, companies, topics"],
     "durability": "timeless|years|months|weeks",
     "use": "how someone running a fleet of AI coding agents would act on it, max 25 words; null when there is no real action",
     "quote": "the speaker's exact words, copied from the transcript",
     "t": "h:mm:ss",
     "strength": 1}
  ],
  "stances": [{"subject": "e.g. Claude Code", "position": "for|against|mixed", "why": "max 20 words", "t": "h:mm:ss",
               "was": "their earlier position if they say it changed, else null"}],
  "skipped": "one line on what you left out and why"
}
```

## Rules
1. Read the whole transcript before writing. About one item per 600 words of transcript (minimum 8, maximum 30), best first.
2. `strength`: 5 = non-obvious and actionable, would change how we work; 3 = useful, known to some; 1 = true but common knowledge.
   Budget: **at most two 5s per video, and no more than a third of items at 4 or 5.** The video's headline is the `thesis`,
   not a strength-5 item.
3. `quote` is copied EXACTLY (join caption lines with single spaces, drop inner timestamps, never fix the wording) and `t` is where it starts.
4. `durability`: would this still be true and useful in 2 years (timeless/years), or does it expire with this model generation (months/weeks)?
5. One idea per item. Before writing, compare every pair of claims: if two say the same thing in different words, keep
   the more specific one. A stance also gets a `stances` line.
6. `claim` states the idea itself, in at most 40 words (count them). Never name the speaker in it: not "Theo says", not "he argues".

## Changed in v2
- Second sweep for how the speaker works (prompts, habits, own tools, own numbers) and changes of mind (`was` on stances). Round 1 missed all of these.
- Item count scales with length (1 per 600 words, 8-30). v1's 8-20 cap starved long videos.
- Strength budget (at most two 5s, at most a third at 4+); the thesis is not an item. v1 had 62% at 4+.
- Explicit duplicate check; claims never name the speaker; `use` may be null.
